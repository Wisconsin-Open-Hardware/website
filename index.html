Mission

Bridging the gap between classroom theory and industry practice by empowering undergraduates with a collaborative, peer-led environment to design, verify, and build industry-grade hardware from scratch.

Vision

To establish UW-Madison as a premier hub for open hardware innovation, making complex hardware design accessible to all motivated students and cultivating world-class engineers who drive real-world progress.

This vision is explicitly informed by the Wisconsin Idea—the philosophy that university research and education should benefit the public and the state beyond the classroom. We position UW-Madison as a feeder school for digital hardware and silicon expertise, ensuring top engineering talent continues to thrive and innovate right here in Wisconsin.

Program Structure

Our onboarding and development structure is designed to take students from fundamental logic gates to complex system integration, regardless of prior experience.

Pre-352 Foundation

Foundational Group

For new members without prior coursework. We cover binary representation, boolean algebra, multiplexers, and basic synchronous registers before merging into project teams.

Track 1

FPGA & Embedded

The default entry point. Covers Training 1 (combinational logic, binary arithmetic, RTL basics) and Training 2 (sequential logic, FSMs, clock management, and simulation-first verification with self-checking testbenches).

Track 2

Tapeout

An advanced track for members who've completed Track 1 or have equivalent proficiency (e.g., ECE 552). Focused on ASIC and tapeout-oriented design methodologies.

Flagship Project

// The WOH Arcade Machine

A full hardware arcade machine combining multiple individually-built games into one unified system, featuring a shared main menu and custom physical peripherals.

  • Development Approach: Members build individual games independently (state-machine driven: start, active, end), which are then integrated into a single VGA-output FPGA system.
  • Sub-Teams: Dedicated teams for each game, plus a peripherals/hardware team focusing on joysticks and controller enclosures.
  • Interdisciplinary: A fantastic entry point not just for ECE students, but for Mechanical Engineering students building physical cabinet hardware.

Leadership

President

Primary liaison to the university, CfLI, ESC, and departmental administration. Directs organizational roadmap, manages faculty advisor relationships, and assists with top-level system integration.

VP of Architecture

Defines architectural specs for core hardware systems, register maps, and bus interconnects. Manages Git repo infrastructure, PR reviews, coding style, verification benches, and co-designs onboarding curriculum.

VP of Implementation

Manages lab logistics and FPGA board allocation. Oversees toolchain workflows (Quartus, ModelSim, Verilator, GTKWave), runs technical workshops, hardware bring-up labs, and coordinates recruitment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need prior Verilog/FPGA experience? +

No! Our tiered onboarding track covers all the fundamentals from scratch, starting with basic logic before moving into advanced topics.

What hardware will I use? +

We primarily use the Terasic DE10-Lite development board featuring an Intel MAX 10 FPGA, utilizing VGA output, switches, buttons, and 7-segment displays for IO.

What is the time commitment? +

Members should expect to commit roughly 3–5 hours per week on average between meetings, trainings, and independent project work.

Who can join? +

The club is open to all majors! While highly relevant for Computer Engineering and Electrical Engineering students, Mechanical Engineering students are also highly encouraged to join, especially for building physical cabinet hardware and peripherals.
